\section{Decomposition of the announced rule's effect}
\label{app:mechanism}

This appendix gives the decompositions behind \cref{sec:res-mechanism}. Every comparison is at
the common state of \cref{tab:mechanism}: the stochastic rest point of the economy without a
backstop, with the quarterly default probability raised to 2\% (1.5\% in brackets). Three kinds
of object appear. Accounting identities hold exactly at a given allocation. Controlled
counterfactuals are allocations that clear one quarter under assumptions no announced policy
would produce, and isolate one channel. Shapley allocations share an effect among interacting
factors by averaging over the order in which they are switched on; they are a convention, not an
identity.

\subsection{The price of capital}
\label{app:mech-capital}

Equation \eqref{eq:capital-price} factors the price of capital exactly as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_{D,t}={}&\underbrace{\frac{\E_t[\Pi^K_{t+1}]}{R_{D,t}}}_{\text{expected payoff}}
\times\underbrace{\frac{\E_t\bigl[\Omega_{D,t,t+1}\,\Pi^K_{t+1}\bigr]}
{\E_t[\Omega_{D,t,t+1}]\,\E_t[\Pi^K_{t+1}]}}_{\text{covariance}}
\times\underbrace{\frac{\E_t[\Omega_{D,t,t+1}]R_{D,t}}{\E_t[\Omega_{D,t,t+1}]R_{D,t}+\lambda_D\mu_{D,t}}}_{\text{constraint premium}},\\
&\Pi^K_{t+1}\equiv mpk_{D,t+1}+(1-\delta_D)\,Q_{D,t+1},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:capital-price-factors}
\end{equation}
where $\Pi^K_{t+1}$ is the payoff on a unit of capital, so the log change in $Q_{D,t}$ is the sum of the log changes in the three factors, with the
expected payoff split between the resale value and the dividend in proportion to their changes.
The identity holds to $10^{-14}$ in every column of \cref{tab:app-capital}.

The source of the higher expected resale value is a controlled counterfactual. Starting from
the economy without a backstop, we replace groups of next-quarter values by those of the policy
economy one group at a time, re-clear the impact quarter exactly, and allocate the change by
Shapley. Of the 0.458\% rise at 2\%, expected capital accumulation and hours next quarter
contribute 0.233 points, expected household demand, the terms of trade and $F$ variables 0.200,
the bond's own continuation price 0.015, the franchise value 0.002, and the impact-quarter
purchase 0.017. Expected capital accumulation alone, switched on by itself, eliminates the whole
115\,bp lending spread.

\subsection{Bank net worth}
\label{app:mech-networth}

Net worth is gross returns on the assets carried into the quarter less deposits owed, plus the
start-up funds of entrants, \eqref{eq:nw-lom}. At the common state the rule raises it by 3.60\%
of no-backstop net worth at 2\% (2.43\% at 1.5\%). The revaluation of capital contributes
3.02 points, of which 3.00 is the identity \eqref{eq:revaluation}: a 0.458\% price rise times
capital worth 6.55 times net worth. The sovereign capital gain adds 0.55, start-up funds 0.04,
and the remaining items less than 0.01. Clearing two quarters jointly gives 3.14\%, of which
capital 2.63 and the sovereign gain 0.48.

From the policy economy's own rest point the same comparison gives 3.30\% of shocked
no-backstop net worth. The difference of $-0.29$ points comes from the initial state: the policy
rest point carries more capital financed with deposits (deposits owed $-0.60$, capital return
$+0.45$) and fewer legacy bonds ($-0.15$). Divided instead by rest-point net worth, which is 14\%
larger, the change is the 2.9 points of \cref{tab:headline-impact}.

\subsection{The constraint}
\label{app:mech-constraint}

Equation \eqref{eq:mu-closed} writes the multiplier as a function of the discount factor, the
deposit rate, net worth and the divertible base. Switching each from its no-backstop to its
policy value and allocating by Shapley, the 115\,bp cut in the lending spread at 2\% splits into
the capital revaluation of net worth ($-107$\,bp), the sovereign capital gain ($-12.5$), the swap
of bonds for reserves banks cannot divert ($-4.5$), the deposit rate ($-2.6$), the franchise
value ($-2.7$), the banker's discount factor ($+3.7$) and the larger divertible base as banks buy
more capital ($+9.1$). At the no-backstop allocation the semi-elasticity of the multiplier in the
price of capital is $\partial\mu_{D,t}/\partial\ln Q_{D,t}=-5.66$: net worth rises 6.62 times as
fast as the price and the divertible base 0.82 times as fast. Holding the allocation at its
no-backstop value, the promise raises the price banks will pay for capital by 0.335\%; in
equilibrium the rise is 0.457\%, an amplification of 1.36.

The surprise purchase's effect on the lending spread, $+3.3$\,bp with one quarter cleared,
combines a slightly lower franchise value next quarter ($+19$\,bp), as banks expect to be better
capitalised, with the reserve swap ($-9$\,bp) and the capital and sovereign gains ($-7$\,bp). With
two quarters cleared the sign reverses ($-4.6$\,bp). Neither sign is established.

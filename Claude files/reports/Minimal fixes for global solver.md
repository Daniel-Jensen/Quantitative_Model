# Shrink the premium, then smooth the kink

The leverage-8 calibration fails for one structural reason, and most of its symptoms follow from it. The D bank earns a large sovereign risk premium in calm times. That premium comes from a default-state franchise value (alpha about 7.5, against 1.3 in the no-default state), which is high because D-bank net worth falls to the 15% floor in default. The premium relaxes the leverage constraint at the rest point: with the instruments at their target values, mu_D is about 1e-4 and the D loan spread is about 2 bp ([worklog](../docs/calibration_worklog_2026-10.md)). To make the constraint bind, the SS spread instrument has to be pushed to 135–140 bp. That is inside the region where the cold start fails (above ~125–200 bp) and close to the point where the deterministic SS stops existing (~240 bp at f = 0.08). In that region the collocation system folds wherever mu switches between 0 and 0.4–0.75 from node to node. So the fix with the best payoff per effort is a model change that costs **zero lines of code**: raise the existing debt-funded recapitalisation floor (`run.NW_FLOOR`, now 0.15) toward the 0.6–1.0 range that the Greek HFSF recapitalisation supports. That attacks the premium at its source and pulls post-default states back toward the box. It needs your approval, because it weakens the risk channel and moves the default event further from Bocola's pure haircut. The computational fixes with the best payoff are, in order: drop the TPI dimensions from the no-TPI grid (a one-line cost cut of ~27%); store smooth arguments instead of the kinked franchise value (~40–60 LOC); overdetermined least squares on the existing LM machinery (~70–100 LOC); and recentre the box on the rest point (~25–40 LOC). None of them removes the economic reason the economy sits near the kink. That is why the model fix comes first and the solver fixes second.

## One mechanism links seven of the eight flaws

Every flaw on the list goes back to where the D bank's constraint switches, and to why the rest point sits near that switch. The current multiplier is `mu = smax(1 − E_Om·(1+rdep)·n/lev, 0, mu_eps)`, and the stored franchise value is `alpha = E_Om·(1+rdep)/(1−mu)` ([point_map.py:330-361](../../global_projection/solver_recursive/point_map.py)). With the hard max, these two lines reduce to **alpha = max(E[Omega]·R, lev/n)**. The stored rule is therefore the maximum of two functions, and it carries the kink of the constraint itself. In default, n_D sits on the 15% floor at most nodes ([recursive_experiment.py:175-178](../../global_projection/solver_recursive/recursive_experiment.py)), so lev/n is large and alpha^d is about 7.5. Every normal-regime node weights that default cell by p^d(s) ([point_map.py:291-304](../../global_projection/solver_recursive/point_map.py)). So the calm-time SDF on the bond payoff tilts five-to-one toward the default branch. That is a sovereign risk premium the D bank collects as carry. At rest its net worth ends up 5–9% above the SS, and its constraint goes slack ([worklog](../docs/calibration_worklog_2026-10.md)).

The rest of the chain is recorded in the worklog. The D-spread instrument has to rise to 135–140 bp for the constraint to bind at rest. The rest-point spread is then steep and non-monotone in the instrument: fitted/cleared 29.7/9.3 bp at 120, 77.5/31.3 at 130, 101.7/163.7 at 140, 74.9/59.9 at 150. At 150 bp the no-shock simulation settles at five different states (75, 159, 53, 4 and 0.1 bp), and the slack ones sit on the box walls. The tensor refinement has no collocation root near the coarse one: the smallest singular value is 5e-6 against 4.2, LM stalls at 3e-3, and every stalled node has a unique pointwise root, so the fold is global rather than local. The worklog places the fold "where high risk meets the bank-state edges": the floor binding in default, and mu_D switching between 0 and 0.4–0.75 in the normal regime ([worklog](../docs/calibration_worklog_2026-10.md)). Time iteration and Newton disagree because the franchise-value recursion contracts at 0.990 per sweep, so the warm start never settles ([guide_global.md:73-76](../docs/guide_global.md)).

This reframes the question. The 2026-08-29 cure, a 100 bp spread so that mu_rest stays away from 0, worked at leverage 5 because nothing relaxed the constraint at rest ([guide_global.md:354-374](../docs/guide_global.md)). At leverage 8 and 14.4% exposure the sovereign carry undoes that cure. Solver fixes can make a kinked problem converge. They cannot stop the calibration from parking the economy on the kink. Hence the ordering below: first shrink what drives the economy to the switch, then make the approximation robust to whatever switching is left.

## Fixes already tried, and what they rule out

The record of failed fixes narrows the search a lot. Before 2026-10-22 the following were tried and kept: a C1 saturation clip at the box edge (`CLIP_EPS = 0.05`); cross-border books fitted in levels; KKT smoothing at `mu_eps = 1e-2` with an SS that solves the same smoothed constraint; a smoothing ladder; an LM fallback in `parsolve`; and an axis-only s-refinement (27 points, square and nested), adopted because the tensor version folds ([worklog](../docs/calibration_worklog_2026-10.md)). The following were rejected, each with a measured reason. A wider smoothing (2e-2) breaks even the coarse grid. A lower box top (p^d 3%) crawls the same way. Smolyak level 2 for the D-bank block (59 points) stalls at ~4e-3. An always-binding constraint gives negative multipliers at 8 of 25 coarse nodes, down to −0.34. A 5x stronger debt-elastic premium (kappa_nfa 0.05) leaves the slow root at 0.987–0.994. The two-box design, built and switched off, failed through the aligned normal box's cold start and a post-default plateau at 7.8e-3 ([recursive_experiment.py:36-42](../../global_projection/solver_recursive/recursive_experiment.py)).

Three conclusions follow. First, more resolution and more smoothing are exhausted: both have been tried in both directions. The numerical literature agrees that smoothing helps Newton but not approximation, because any smoothing replaces a kink with a high-curvature band that a global low-order polynomial still has to fit ([MMW 2021](https://web.stanford.edu/~maliars/Files/JME2021.pdf)). Second, the failed kappa test suggests that the slow mode sits on the bank side (the deposit position as the counterpart of D-bank net worth), not on the external side. That fits Schmitt-Grohé and Uribe's result that the closing devices give near-identical dynamics ([SGU 2003](https://www.nber.org/papers/w9270)). Third, the always-binding failure shows the D bank is genuinely slack over a third of the box, so any fix that assumes the constraint binds everywhere is ruled out.

### Rank 0: four diagnostics that decide everything else

The cheapest work is measurement. A 2-D grid of deterministic SS solves over (f, SS spread) at leverage 8 maps the existence frontier, which the repo now knows only as a single point (~240 bp at f = 0.08) and as the hint in `_check_targets` to "raise f until leverage is the least root" ([steady_state.py:184-196](../../global_projection/steady_state.py)). The eigenvector of the 0.987–0.994 root tells you whether a bank-side stationarity device (rank 9) can work. Tracking sigma_min along the existing ladders separates a turning point, which continuation can follow and which may signal another branch of equilibria, from a refined grid that simply has no root nearby. Least squares (rank 4) addresses the second case; continuation does not. Starting the Newton from a converged TI limit is the selection test that Rendahl's convergence result motivates: under broad conditions TI converges to the true solution even with occasionally binding constraints ([Rendahl 2015](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/255270?show=full)). Note that his theorem covers single-agent Euler systems. It does not automatically extend to a two-country GE with banks. The prerequisite for every model choice below is the floor sweep: rest-point spread, mu_rest, alpha^d and the post-default state distance, at NW_FLOOR = 0.15/0.4/0.6/0.8/1.0. The repo has no such measurement ([codebase notes, §7 gaps](../research_notes/Minimal%20fixes%20for%20global%20solver/codebase_mapping.md)).

### Rank 1: a data-backed recapitalisation removes the premium at its source

Greece recapitalised its banks after PSI to a **9% Core Tier 1 target** ([Eurobank H1 2012](https://athexgroup.gr/en/documents/10180/43442/Financial%20Report%20EUROBANK%20ERGASIAS%20S.A.%20%282012%2CSix-Month%20Statement%2CBoth%29.pdf/c5ef1103-5b7c-49f1-9354-cdb8f2be7a6b)). The losses were about **EUR 37.7bn** from PSI plus related loans ([Eurobank Macro Focus, Jan 2013](https://www.eurobank.gr/Uploads/Reports/GREECE%20MACRFOCUS%20%20January%2025%202013.pdf)), against a **EUR 50bn HFSF envelope** ([Eurobank investor presentation](https://www.athexgroup.gr/documents/10180/43440/64_12407_2012_English_2.pdf/d5fb4620-a17f-4b55-9326-7ebe66e14185)), and Eurobank's need was described as "commensurate with PSI losses" ([Eurobank filing](https://www.athexgroup.gr/documents/10180/43440/278_26554_2012_English_2.pdf/8f5312e2-765d-4d2e-b1f2-57864feb6228)). Recapitalisation was close to full, and it was paid for with public debt (EFSF). The model already has that mechanism. `_funded_floor` tops D-bank net worth up to `nw_floor_frac·n_ss` and funds it with new D debt ([point_map.py:178-203](../../global_projection/solver_recursive/point_map.py)). So the change is a parameter in code that already exists and is already tested (N5). Raising the floor lowers alpha^d toward alpha^nd, which shrinks the calm-time SDF tilt toward default. The pass-through on the no-default IRF still runs through the mark-to-market loss, which is Bocola's object. The likely result is that mu binds at rest with the instrument back near 100 bp, inside the convergent region.

There are three costs, and all three are why this needs your approval. The first is the economics. Banks that expect to be made whole fear default less, so the risk channel weakens. Hur, Sosa-Padilla and Yom find that larger bailouts relax financial frictions but raise fiscal needs and default risk ([NBER w28412](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28412/w28412.pdf)), and Pérez finds that post-default bailouts are desirable ex post but reduce welfare ex ante ([SIEPR](https://siepr.stanford.edu/publications/working-paper/sovereign-debt-domestic-banks-and-provision-public-liquidity)). The HFSF's actual terms (common shares, with warrants to private investors) diluted incumbents ([Alpha Bank Q3 2012](https://www.athexgroup.gr/documents/10180/43440/64_26366_2012_English_2.pdf/1f379013-8de7-45f0-a82e-1b4587cf53e9)). That argues for a partial chi, or for a dilution factor on alpha^d, so that some precautionary motive survives. The second is the box. The top-up is new D debt that the D banks absorb, so post-default b_DD′ rises even as P_D and n_D move closer to the box. The bond dimension could then move further outside ([codebase notes, §7](../research_notes/Minimal%20fixes%20for%20global%20solver/codebase_mapping.md)). The floor sweep has to measure all three distances. The third is the framing. The design is "one pure-haircut feared event" ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)), and the 15% floor already departs from it. Raising the floor is a larger departure from the same baseline, not a new one, and the paper has to say so.

### Ranks 2–3: a free cost cut, then a smoother stored object

Dropping M_cb and O_cb from the no-TPI grid takes one line, because `_multi_indices` already gives a dimension with `mu_vec[j] = 0` a single centre node of degree 0. The M/O bands are symmetric round 0, and with the TPI off, x_cb = 0 keeps the economy on the M = O = 0 slice ([state_grid.py:40-54](../../global_projection/solver_recursive/state_grid.py), [point_map.py:123-136](../../global_projection/solver_recursive/point_map.py)). The unknowns fall from 1080 to 920 on the refined grid. The TPI then needs a ~10-line re-seed onto the full grid through the existing `_seed_from`. This is a cost fix, not a robustness fix: the folds sit at the high-risk × bank-state edges, not at the M/O nodes.

The smoother stored object is the literature's strongest remedy for kinks. Christiano and Fisher found that approximating the smooth conditional expectation, and recovering the multiplier from it exactly, dominated the alternatives for occasionally binding constraints ([NBER t0218](https://www.nber.org/papers/t0218)). Their method is still the benchmark that OccBin is judged against ([Guerrieri-Iacoviello 2015](https://www.matteoiacoviello.com/research_files/JME_2015.pdf)). Here the minimal version follows from the identity alpha = max(E[Omega]·R, lev/n). Replace the DERIVED rules `alpha_D/F` with `EOm_D/F` and `phi_D/F = lev/n`, and rebuild alpha′ in `_discount_kernels` with the same smooth max. Readers of `o["alpha_D"]` are unaffected ([decision_rules.py:9-21](../../global_projection/solver_recursive/decision_rules.py), [recursive_experiment.py:558-560](../../global_projection/solver_recursive/recursive_experiment.py)). The cheaper alternative is a Garcia-Zangwill unknown for the IC, which the code already uses for the TPI floor ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)). The gain is partial, and the reason matters. The quadrature runs over s′ and the regime only, and next-period bank states are deterministic given today's choices ([point_map.py:258-272](../../global_projection/solver_recursive/point_map.py)). So E[Omega] is smooth in s but in the bank-state directions it is a sum of ten shifted kinks, which is exactly where the folds sit. phi = lev/n also turns steep where n is on the floor, which is one more reason to do rank 1 first. A full parameterised-expectations redesign (storing E[Omega·payoff]) would remove the kink properly, at 100+ LOC.

### Ranks 4–7: robustness the solver can buy

Least squares turns "no root" into a measurable minimum. The sum of squares always has a minimiser on a compact coefficient set, and when there are more evaluation points than coefficients, the extra rows add rank where the square system is singular. The handbook warns that least squares can itself be ill-conditioned ([Fernández-Villaverde et al.](https://www.hoover.org/sites/default/files/fernandez-rubio-shorfheide-hbofmacro_solveestimate_v1.pdf)), and stability results for discrete polynomial least squares need roughly quadratic oversampling ([arXiv 1401.0894](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.0894)). The 115-point tensor nodes already contain the 27 axis nodes, and both the FD Jacobian and the SVD-based `_lm_step` handle rectangular matrices. One trap: `lu_factor` accepts a rectangular J, and `lu_solve` then raises an uncaught `_flapack.error`. So `_factor` needs a one-line shape guard ([collocation.py:180-195](../../global_projection/solver_recursive/collocation.py)). The real cost is methodological. max|F| ≤ 1e-9 can no longer be met, so `solve_ok`, and with it `calibrate_stochastic`'s Broyden, needs a new acceptance definition. Equation classes also need weights through the existing `scale` vector. That definition is yours to approve.

Recentring reuses machinery that already exists. `build_state_box(at=…)` shifts the seven bank stocks, and the box travels with the rules into `_walk_instruments` and `_refine_s` ([state_grid.py:278-283](../../global_projection/solver_recursive/state_grid.py)). Ergodic-set domains cut maximum Euler errors by about an order of magnitude at a fixed number of coefficients ([JMMV 2014](https://lmaliar.ws.gc.cuny.edu/files/2019/01/JEDC2014_JMMV.pdf)). The same paper warns that rotating a μ=1 grid can lower accuracy, because μ=1 has no cross terms, so translate the box but do not rotate it. Starting each round from the previous rest point rather than the SS would depart from your recorded convention ([worklog](../docs/calibration_worklog_2026-10.md)). Anderson acceleration improves the rate of linearly convergent fixed points, not of quadratically convergent ones ([arXiv 2109.14176](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.14176)). At 0.99 per sweep, plain TI needs about 2,000 sweeps, so Anderson has to bring that to 100–300 to compete with a Newton. Its value is as an equilibrium selector, which is a modelling decision. The wide post-default box (rank 7) keeps the V5 normal box that converges and changes only the post set. The documented failure came from the aligned normal box's cold start. Plateaus like 7.8e-3 could still recur at wide nodes, where the guards bind.

## Changes that cost the story more than they save

Four candidates fix a flaw by changing what the paper is about. Lowering the rest-point hazard to 0.05%/qtr shrinks the premium proportionally, but the current 0.1% is set directly from the Greek 1-year CDS in 2008Q3 (0.092%) ([calibrate_stochastic.py:205-206](../../global_projection/solver_recursive/calibrate_stochastic.py)). The basis-adjusted 0.02–0.05% range has no verified source. It also puts the 2% headline shock at ~94% of the way to the box top unless `S_COVER_SD` is raised. A constant intermediation cost tau lets the shadow part of the wedge shrink. Done alone, it pushes mu_rest back toward the kink and reopens the accuracy problem the 100 bp choice cured, so it is useful only after rank 1. A risk-neutral outside pricer of the D bond caps the premium at expected loss. It also detaches Q_bD from Greek bank net worth and changes what the TPI competes against, which conflicts with the finding that price impact runs mostly through the continuation ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)). Broner, Erce, Martin and Ventura's creditor discrimination, where domestic banks earn more in turbulent times, argues for keeping the domestic banks as the pricer ([ESM/JME](https://www.esm.europa.eu/publications/sovereign-debt-markets-turbulent-times-creditor-discrimination-and-crowding-out-effects)). Calibrating to ergodic means sidesteps the non-unique rest point. With rho_s = 0.95, however, 2,000 draws give about 50 effective observations, so Monte Carlo error would exceed the 0.1 bp Broyden tolerance by about an order of magnitude. Local adaptive sparse grids ([Brumm-Scheidegger 2017](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/2017/09/01/using-adaptive-sparse-grids-solve-high-dimensional-dynamic)) and deep-learning solvers are the textbook answers to kinks, but both are rewrites, and the deep-learning accuracy is ~1e-3.

Some gaps remain open. Bocola's appendix and replication code could not be fetched, so how he solves the post-default continuation is unverified. Pre-PSI aggregate Greek bank equity, which would pin chi exactly, was not found. Nothing in the repo measures how the premium responds to the floor or to p^d at leverage 8.

## Conclusion

The flaws look numerical, but they are caused by a calibration interaction. At leverage 8 and 14.4% exposure, the default-state franchise value turns sovereign carry into slack at rest. That forces the spread instrument into the fold zone, so every solver fix so far has been fighting a kink the calibration keeps re-creating. The right sequence is therefore: measure first (the floor sweep and the existence frontier, about a day of compute); then decide on chi with those numbers in hand; then add the smooth stored object and least squares for whatever switching remains. If a partial, dilutive recapitalisation brings the D-spread instrument back near 100 bp, the cold-start and existence limits stop binding without further solver work, and the HFSF anchor gives the paper a defensible Greek institution in place of an arbitrary 15% floor.

The decision with the most consequence is not technical. Your previous rulings already settle the rest point (reached from the SS) and the constraint at rest (binding). What is still open is how much of the fear of default the model should keep. A full recap makes the model easy to solve but muted. A pure haircut is Bocola-faithful but, at this calibration, cannot be solved. The chi in between is a calibration target in its own right.
